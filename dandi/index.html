The Problem

You have the goal. Life gets in the way.

The things we want to do for ourselves are easy to put off when there’s always something more urgent competing for our attention.

“Nearly 1 in 2 people who make New Year’s resolutions have given up on them by the beginning of February.”

UCLA Health, 2024

Research & Ideation

The challenge wasn’t wanting the goal. It was following through.

Research reinforced how easily long-term goals get pushed aside: 69% of Americans said the pace of society makes it harder to stick with long-term goals, while 26% said they often or always feel too busy to even think about them (Northwestern Mutual, 2013).

Planning also made a meaningful difference. In a study of 248 people, 35% of participants who received a motivational intervention exercised, compared with 91% of those who also made a specific plan for when and where they would act (Milne et al., 2002).

That pointed me toward two needs: make the next step easier to define, and make goals harder to forget.

The idea behind Dandi

I wanted those goals to have a place that felt worth coming back to — not another productivity dashboard. The idea of wishing on a dandelion became the foundation for Dandi: starting with something you hope to make happen, then giving it the structure and space to grow into something real.

The Solution

Make big goals easier to start, easier to remember, and easier to keep moving.

Dandi gives people one place to turn big goals into smaller, manageable pieces and keep track of the progress they’re making along the way. Because progress can look different for every goal, Dandi offers customizability. Users can choose from tasks, milestones, trackers, reminders, and notes instead of being forced into one system.

The goal was to make figuring out what to do next feel simpler, while keeping the things you want to make happen visible enough that they don’t get lost when life gets busy.

Outcome & Reflections

Big goals feel less intimidating. And harder to forget.

Early beta feedback reinforced that Dandi made it easier to turn a goal into something users could actually start and come back to. 4 of 6 beta testers said Dandi made it easier to figure out what to do next toward a goal, while 3 of 6 said the app surfaced tasks they had forgotten about.

Building Dandi changed how I think about what I can make as a designer.

I truly enjoyed being able to move from idea to implementation quickly. With Claude & Figma MCP, I could easily explore ideas that previously would've felt too technical or out of scope. I also ended up pulling inspiration from places I didn’t expect — especially Pinterest, which I use in my day-to-day life but never thought of as part of my design process.

The harder part was knowing when to stop. With AI, testing multiple visual directions became easier than ever, which also made it easier to second-guess my decisions. I could get pulled into polishing details during moments when my priority needed to be usability. It taught me to set clearer priorities, time-box exploration, and recognize when a design was ready to move forward.

Dandi is in the process of iOS App Store approval!

In the meantime, I'll be working with beta users & improving the experience :)
